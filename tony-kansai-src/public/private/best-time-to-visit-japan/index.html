When is the best time to visit Japan?

Updated November 3, 2026 · by Mario, private guide in Japan

Photo: Midori · CC BY 3.0

The short answer

Spring and autumn are the best times to visit Japan. Cherry blossoms usually peak in Tokyo and Kyoto in late March and early April, and autumn color peaks in Kyoto from mid-November to early December. Winter brings the clearest views of Mount Fuji. Avoid Golden Week (29 April to 5 May), Obon in mid-August and the New Year holidays if you can.

Cherry blossom season 2027

Cherry blossoms usually open in Tokyo and Kyoto in late March, reaching full bloom about a week later and lasting roughly a week, depending on rain and wind. Official forecasts for 2027 will be published from early in the year, and the exact peak can shift by a week or more. To be safe, plan for the last days of March and the first days of April, and book hotels, restaurants and guides months ahead: this is the busiest season of the year.

  • Early starts are essential. Famous spots such as the Philosopher’s Path, Maruyama Park and Chidorigafuchi are calmest at sunrise.
  • Follow the bloom north and up. If you miss the peak in Kyoto, the Fuji Five Lakes and the mountains bloom a little later.

Autumn color

Autumn color arrives first in the mountains and the north. Around Lake Chuzenji in Nikko it peaks in October; in Hakone in early to mid-November; and in Kyoto and Tokyo from mid-November to early December. Kyoto in the last two weeks of November is one of the most beautiful sights in Japan, and also among the busiest. Temples such as Tofuku-ji, Eikan-do and Kiyomizu-dera hold evening illuminations, and Mario plans the day so you see the best of both morning and night.

Winter: the quiet, clear season

From December to February, the air is dry and the skies are often clear. Mount Fuji is most likely to be visible on winter mornings, gardens are calm, and onsen and ryokan feel at their best. Kyoto in January is quiet, with occasional snow on temple roofs. It is an underrated time for a first visit, especially for couples.

Summer

July and August are hot and humid across most of Japan, often above 33°C in Tokyo and Kyoto. With early starts, indoor afternoons and evening walks, summer can still be enjoyable, and it is the season for festivals and climbing Mount Fuji. The rainy season, from early June to mid-July, brings frequent showers but also beautiful hydrangeas and fewer visitors.

Japanese holidays to avoid

  • Golden Week, 29 April to 5 May: a cluster of public holidays when much of Japan travels. Trains and hotels fill and prices rise.
  • Obon, mid-August: families return to their hometowns; the shinkansen is crowded around 13 to 16 August.
  • New Year, about 29 December to 3 January: many restaurants and some sights close, and shrines are extremely busy in the first days of January.

Our recommendation

For a first visit, aim for late March to mid-April, or mid-October to early December. If you prefer fewer crowds and clear skies, consider January, February or late May. Whatever the season, the same rule applies: start early, book the best restaurants and experiences well ahead, and leave room to adapt to the weather.

Questions

Frequently asked

When do cherry blossoms bloom in Japan in 2027?

In Tokyo and Kyoto they typically bloom from late March to early April. Official 2027 forecasts are published early in the year, and the exact dates vary with the weather.

When is the best time to see autumn leaves in Kyoto?

From mid-November to early December, with the peak usually in the last two weeks of November.

When is Mount Fuji most visible?

In late autumn and winter, especially on clear early mornings. In summer it is often hidden by cloud.

What are the busiest times to visit Japan?

Cherry blossom season, Golden Week (29 April to 5 May), Obon in mid-August, late November in Kyoto and the New Year holidays.
