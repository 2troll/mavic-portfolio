Hiroshima & Miyajima

Highlights with Mario

  • Atomic Bomb Dome

    The ruin of the Industrial Promotion Hall, left as it stood after 6 August 1945, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site at the edge of the Peace Memorial Park.

  • Peace Memorial Museum

    A moving, carefully documented museum. Mario allows one and a half to two hours and paces the day so there is time to reflect afterward.

  • Itsukushima Shrine

    Shrine halls built over the water on Miyajima, joined by vermilion corridors. At high tide the whole shrine seems to float.

  • The great torii

    The famous gate standing in the sea, fully restored in 2022. At low tide you can walk out to its base; at high tide it rises from the water.

  • Omotesando and Mount Misen

    Miyajima’s shopping street, with grilled oysters and maple-leaf cakes, and a ropeway toward the summit of Mount Misen for those with time.

A suggested day

How the day could unfold

A starting point, not a script: Mario adjusts the order and pace to the season, the crowds and your interests.

  1. 07:30Hotel lobby pick-up in Kyoto or Osaka and a reserved Nozomi shinkansen to Hiroshima.
  2. 09:30The Peace Memorial Park, the Atomic Bomb Dome and the Children’s Peace Monument.
  3. 10:30The Peace Memorial Museum, unhurried.
  4. 12:30Lunch of Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki, layered with noodles and cabbage.
  5. 14:00Train to Miyajimaguchi and the ten-minute ferry to the island.
  6. 15:00Itsukushima Shrine and the great torii, timed around the tide, then Omotesando.
  7. 18:00Ferry and shinkansen back, arriving in Kyoto or Osaka in the evening.

Insider tips

What Mario wants you to know

  • Never split Hiroshima and Miyajima over separate days from Kyoto or Osaka; together they make one complete, balanced day.
  • Mario checks the tide table: high tide for the floating shrine, low tide to walk to the torii.
  • Miyajima charges a small visitor tax (¥100) on the ferry fare, and Itsukushima Shrine charges a modest admission.
  • Reserved shinkansen seats matter on this route; Mario books them, and you pay the fares at cost.
