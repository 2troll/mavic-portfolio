TikTok’s sakura: Kyoto and Osaka in spring

Cherry blossom lasts barely two weeks and is the most filmed moment of the year in Japan. You need to know where and when to go to avoid crowds.

Late March · early April

Okazaki Canal

The shot
Cherry trees over the canal, sometimes with boats.
When to go
Early morning at full bloom.
Guide’s tip
From the bridge you can film the tunnel of blossom over the water.

Osaka Castle

The shot
The white and gold castle surrounded by cherry trees.
When to go
At park opening or at sunset.
Guide’s tip
Shoot from the moat: castle above, blossoms below.

Kiyomizu-dera in bloom

The shot
The stage above pink cherry treetops.
When to go
First thing or at the spring night illumination.
Guide’s tip
Come on a weekday: peak-bloom weekends are impossible.

Kimono among cherry blossoms

The shot
A kimono walk through Higashiyama’s slopes in bloom.
When to go
Very early; book the kimono in advance.
Guide’s tip
Kimono rentals fill up in spring: I’ll help you book.

Deer and sakura in Nara

The shot
Deer lying under the park’s cherry trees.
When to go
In the morning, before tour groups.
Guide’s tip
The winning shot: a deer and a falling petal. Patience.

Arashiyama in spring

The shot
Hills dotted with cherry trees above the Katsura River.
When to go
In the morning; the bamboo before 8:00.
Guide’s tip
The river boats give you a shot nobody else has.

Hidden gems across Japan · Spring

What people save… and almost nobody visits

Gardens, flowers and landscapes from all over Japan that show up in saved videos, not on the usual tours.

  1. Chūreitō Pagoda and Mt Fuji

    Yamanashi · early to mid April

    Why people save it
    The red pagoda, cherry blossoms and Mount Fuji in one shot.
    Getting there
    Climb the nearly 400 steps at sunrise: Fuji is usually clearest in the morning.

  2. Mount Yoshino

    Nara · early to mid April

    Why people save it
    Thousands of cherry trees blooming up the mountain in layers.
    Getting there
    Kintetsu train from Osaka. Arrive early: blossoms climb from bottom to top over two weeks.

  3. Shirahige’s lake torii

    Shiga (Lake Biwa) · all year, best at sunrise

    Why people save it
    A red torii standing in the water of Japan’s largest lake.
    Getting there
    JR Kosei line from Kyoto. Don’t cross the road for the photo: there is a safe viewpoint.

  4. Motonosumi Inari

    Yamaguchi · all year

    Why people save it
    Over a hundred red torii running down to cliffs above the sea.
    Getting there
    Best by car; it is far from everything, which is why it is nearly empty.

  5. Takachiho Gorge

    Miyazaki · all year

    Why people save it
    A waterfall dropping into an emerald river between rock walls, seen from a rowing boat.
    Getting there
    Boats are booked and fill up fast; reserve as soon as you know your date.

  6. Ine no Funaya

    Northern Kyoto · all year

    Why people save it
    Wooden boathouses built over the water, with boats parked underneath.
    Getting there
    Pair it with Amanohashidate on the same day; about 2–3 hours from Kyoto.
