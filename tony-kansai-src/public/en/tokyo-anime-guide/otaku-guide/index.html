Iheb · The otaku guide

The complete otaku guide to Tokyo

Every anime, manga, card and retro-game district explained: what each one is for, when to go, the shop rules nobody tells you, and how to get it all home.

Plan my otaku day with Iheb

The best otaku areas in Tokyo are Akihabara (cards, figures, retro games), Nakano Broadway (rare collectibles) and Ikebukuro (Animate, Pokémon Center Mega Tokyo, Otome Road). Add Shibuya PARCO for Nintendo TOKYO, Tokyo Station’s Character Street, and The Gundam Base Tokyo in Odaiba. Iheb, a pop-culture and Pokémon card specialist who lives in Japan, plans the order, the trains and the shops for your group.

Shop rules nobody tells you

  • Photos: many shops ban photos of their cases. Look for the sign, or ask.
  • Floors: the best shops are often on the 4th floor and up. The ground floor is the tourist floor.
  • Cash: most big shops take cards, but small stalls and capsule machines need cash and coins.
  • Tax-free: bring your passport, keep every receipt, and keep goods sealed until you leave Japan.
  • Carry less: leave bags in a station coin locker and shop with your hands free.
  • Getting it home: post big boxes home by EMS, or send your suitcase to the airport.

FAQ

What is the best area in Tokyo for anime fans?

Akihabara is the classic start (cards, figures, retro games). Collectors also love Nakano Broadway, and Ikebukuro is the hub for anime goods, Animate and Otome Road. Most fans need at least one full day for these three.

Can I take photos inside anime and card shops?

Often not. Many shops in Akihabara and Nakano ban photos of their display cases. Look for the sign or ask first; Iheb tells you shop by shop.

Is tax-free shopping available for tourists?

Yes, at shops that display the Tax-Free sign: bring your passport, keep the receipts and do not open the goods before you leave Japan, because the refund is processed on departure.

How do I get big purchases home?

Many shops pack figures well; for large boxes you can send them home by Japan Post (EMS) or forward your suitcase to the airport with a luggage delivery service.

When is Comiket?

Comic Market is usually held twice a year, in August and December, at Tokyo Big Sight. It is huge and very crowded; check the official dates first.
