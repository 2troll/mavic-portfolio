How to take a taxi in Japan

Where to get one

Stations and hotels have taxi ranks (タクシー乗り場). On the street, just raise your hand. The sign by the windscreen says 空車 when the taxi is free; 賃走 or 実車, taken; 迎車, on its way to pick someone up; and 回送, not taking passengers.

The door opens by itself

The driver opens and closes the rear left door with a lever. Wait for it to open, and don’t shut it yourself when you get out.

Show the destination

Many drivers speak little English, and a name said with a foreign accent is easy to misunderstand. With the name in Japanese (or the map) there’s no doubt. Seat belts are compulsory in the back seats too.

How to pay

You pay what the meter shows: no haggling and no tipping. Most city taxis take cards, and many also take IC cards such as ICOCA or Suica; check the stickers on the window or ask when you get in, and carry some cash just in case. From 22:00 the fare goes up.

Groups and luggage

A standard taxi takes four passengers; if you’re more, take two. Suitcases go in the trunk: the driver opens it and usually helps you load them.

Taxi apps

GO is Japan’s most used taxi app: it’s in English, lets you sign up with a foreign phone number and pay in the app. Check before the trip that your card works with it.

Taxi or train?

For short rides within Kyoto or Osaka, especially for three or four people, a taxi is comfortable. Between cities or to the airport, the train is usually faster and much cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

Do you tip taxi drivers in Japan?

No. You pay what the meter shows and that’s it. If you leave extra, you’ll normally be given the change back.

Can I pay for a taxi by card?

In most taxis in Kyoto, Osaka and Kobe, yes, and many take ICOCA or Suica too. Check the stickers on the window or ask 「カードで払えますか？」 before you set off, and carry some cash just in case.

Why isn’t saying the name in English enough?

Because many places have a Japanese name that sounds nothing like the English one (the Golden Pavilion is 金閣寺, Kinkaku-ji), and because some names are alike: Osaka has several Namba stations run by different companies. Read in Japanese, the driver doesn’t hesitate.

What do I type if my hotel isn’t on the list?

The hotel’s name or address, in Japanese if possible: it’s in your booking confirmation, on the hotel’s Japanese website or on the hotel card from the front desk. If you only have it in Latin letters, type it anyway and show the map too.

How many people fit in a taxi?

A standard taxi takes four passengers. If you’re five or six, the simplest option is two taxis.

Does the GO app work for tourists?

Yes: it’s in English and lets you sign up with a foreign phone number and pay by card in the app. Some foreign cards fail, so test it before you rely on it.

How do I get to a mosque by taxi?

The list includes the Osaka Ibaraki mosque (大阪茨木モスク) and the Kobe mosque (神戸ムスリムモスク), with their addresses in Japanese. For the Kyoto mosque and prayer spaces at the sights, see the Kansai mosques page.

Taxi or train from Kyoto to Nara or the airport?

Train. On long trips it’s usually faster and much cheaper than a taxi. A taxi pays off for short distances within the city.
